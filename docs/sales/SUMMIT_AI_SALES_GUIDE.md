## 0. Read This First

If you only read one page, read this one.

### What Summit Is

Summit AI Services is an AI-powered operations layer for dental offices.

That means it helps the dental team organize, draft, review, and follow up on the work that surrounds patient care:

- Clinical notes.
- Morning huddles.
- Front desk tasks.
- Scheduling.
- Recall.
- Patient forms.
- Patient communication.
- Treatment follow-up.
- Insurance.
- Claim review.
- Pre-authorizations.
- Billing.
- Payment plans.
- Reports.
- Inventory.
- Compliance tasks.
- Staff workflow questions.

### The Simple Pitch

Use this when someone asks, "What do you do?"

> Summit helps dental offices reduce admin overload by turning notes, front desk work, billing, recall, treatment follow-up, and daily practice tasks into organized AI-assisted workflows.

Shorter:

> Summit helps dental teams finish the work that falls through the cracks.

Even shorter:

> Summit is an AI operations layer for dental practices.

### What Summit Does Not Do

This matters. Do not oversell.

- Summit does not replace the dentist.
- Summit does not replace the office manager.
- Summit does not replace Open Dental.
- Summit does not guarantee HIPAA compliance.
- Summit does not guarantee insurance reimbursement.
- Summit does not make final clinical decisions.
- Summit does not magically fix a broken office without workflow adoption.

The safest sentence:

> Summit assists the team. The practice still reviews, approves, and owns the final decision.

### The Main Buyer Pain

Dental offices are busy small healthcare businesses. Most of the pain comes from too many small workflows happening at once:

- The doctor has notes to finish.
- The front desk has phones, forms, reminders, cancellations, recall, and balances.
- The billing person has claims, pre-auths, attachments, denials, and patient AR.
- The office manager has to keep the day moving without clean visibility.
- The owner wants more production, better collections, less stress, and fewer missed opportunities.

Summit is sold against that mess.

### The Best Opening Question

Ask this early:

> Where is your team most overloaded right now: notes, front desk work, billing, recall, treatment follow-up, or general practice visibility?

That question routes the entire sales call.

| "Insurance is killing us" | Claim rework, denials, pre-auths, AR | Ask about denial reasons, attachments, narratives, and aging |

| "We need better systems" | Broad operations pain | Ask which workflow breaks most often |
| "We are growing" | Standardization and visibility pain | Ask about locations, provider count, reporting, and training |
| "We already have software" | Fear of another tool | Ask which workflow their current software still does not solve |
| "I do not trust AI" | Risk concern | Emphasize assistive AI, human review, and final provider approval |

### The Golden Rule

Do not demo everything.

The product has many modules. Showing every module makes the product feel complicated. A good rep finds the pain first, then demos only the workflow that matches the pain.

### The 5 Sentences Every Rep Must Memorize

1. Summit is an AI operations layer for dental practices.
2. It helps organize notes, front desk work, billing, recall, treatment follow-up, and daily practice visibility.
3. Summit assists the team; the practice still reviews, approves, and owns final decisions.
4. Open Dental remains the system of record; Summit supports the workflows around it.
5. The right rollout starts with one painful workflow, not every module at once.

## 1. What You Are Really Selling

You are not selling "AI."

Dental buyers do not wake up thinking, "I need artificial intelligence." They wake up thinking:

- "My front desk is drowning."
- "My doctor is still charting after 6 PM."
- "We have overdue recall patients."
- "Claims keep coming back."
- "Treatment plans are not getting scheduled."
- "I do not know what the team is missing."
- "We need more capacity, but hiring is expensive."

Summit should be sold as practical operating leverage.

### The Core Promise

> Summit helps dental teams see the work, prioritize the work, draft the repetitive parts, and keep follow-up moving.

That is the cleanest value.

### The Product In One Mental Model

Think of a dental office like an airport.

The dentist is flying the plane. The front desk is the gate, ticket counter, and customer support desk. Billing is baggage claim and finance. The office manager is air traffic control.

Most practices do not need more random screens. They need air traffic control.

Summit is that layer:

- What is happening today?
- Which patients need attention?
- Which claims are risky?
- Which balances need follow-up?
- Which treatment plans are unscheduled?
- Which patients might not come back?
- Which notes need review?
- Which forms are missing?
- Which team member needs help?

Do not use the airport analogy on every sales call. Use it for yourself so the product is easier to understand.

### The Better Way To Talk About AI

Bad:

> Our AI automates your dental office.

Good:

> Summit uses AI to support the parts of the day that are repetitive, easy to miss, or hard to prioritize.

Bad:

> The AI handles the clinical note.

Good:

> The AI drafts the structure. The provider reviews, edits, and approves.

Bad:

> The AI fixes claims.

Good:

> The AI helps flag common claim risks before the billing team submits or follows up.

Bad:

> The AI replaces staff.

Good:

> Summit helps the staff you already have work from clearer lists and better drafts.

## 3. Dental Office Basics For New Reps

This section teaches enough dental context to avoid sounding lost.

### The Main People In A Dental Office

Owner dentist:
Owns the business and often treats patients. Cares about profit, production, clinical quality, patient experience, staff performance, and time.

Associate dentist:
Treats patients but may not own the practice. Cares about workflow, clinical quality, notes, schedule, and handoffs.

Hygienist:
Performs cleanings, perio maintenance, charting, patient education, X-rays in many offices, and recall-related care. Hygienists are very important to repeat patient revenue.

Dental assistant:
Supports the dentist during procedures, room setup, sterilization, imaging, supplies, charting support, and patient handoffs.

Office manager:
Runs the business side. Cares about schedule, staff, collections, claims, reports, patient experience, systems, and whether work is being completed.

Front desk:
Answers phones, schedules patients, handles forms, confirmations, reminders, insurance intake, balances, patient questions, and communication.

Billing / insurance coordinator:
Handles claims, insurance verification, pre-auths, attachments, narratives, denials, payments, and patient balances.

Treatment coordinator:
Explains treatment plans, costs, insurance estimates, financing, and follow-up. Not every office has a dedicated treatment coordinator.

### The Main Money Terms

Production:
The value of dentistry performed or scheduled.

Collections:
Money actually collected from patients and insurance.

AR:
Accounts receivable. Money owed to the practice.

Patient AR:
Money patients owe.

Insurance AR:
Money insurance companies owe.

Aging:
How old unpaid balances or claims are. 30, 60, and 90 days matter.

Write-off:
Money the practice cannot collect because of insurance contracts or adjustments.

Fee schedule:
The practice's list of prices for procedures.

UCR:
Usual, customary, and reasonable fee benchmarks.

### The Main Clinical And Workflow Terms

Open Dental:
A popular dental practice management system. Summit is positioned as an operations layer around Open Dental-style workflows, not a replacement.

SOAP note:
Clinical documentation format: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan.

CDT code:
Dental procedure code. Examples: D1110 for adult cleaning, D2750 for a crown.

Recall:
Getting patients back for hygiene, perio maintenance, or continuing care.

Perio:
Gum health. Perio disease can require special treatment and charting.

Treatment plan:
Recommended procedures, codes, phases, and costs.

Unscheduled treatment:
Treatment the doctor recommended but the patient has not scheduled.

Pre-authorization:
Insurance review before treatment. It estimates coverage but does not always guarantee payment.

Claim:
Bill sent to insurance after treatment.

Denial:
Insurance refuses or delays payment.

Narrative:
Written explanation attached to a claim.

Attachment:
Supporting document, such as an X-ray, perio chart, image, or clinical note.

No-show:
Patient misses appointment.

Chair utilization:
How effectively the practice fills treatment chairs.

### How A Normal Dental Day Gets Messy

Morning:

- Team checks the schedule.
- Front desk confirms patients.
- Insurance may need verification.
- Some patients have balances.
- Some patients are high no-show risk.
- Some patients have unscheduled treatment to discuss.
- Some forms may be missing.

During the day:

- Doctors and hygienists treat patients.
- Assistants support procedures.
- Notes need to be written.
- Treatment plans need to be presented.
- Patients ask insurance and payment questions.
- Emergencies or cancellations disrupt the schedule.

After visits:

- Notes need approval.
- Claims need submission.
- Narratives and attachments may be needed.
- Follow-ups may need to be sent.
- Balances may need statements.
- Recall lists need attention.

That is why Summit exists.

### Common Dental Scenarios And How Summit Fits

Scenario 1: The patient cancels a long appointment tomorrow.

What the office feels:

- Lost production.
- A sudden schedule hole.
- Manual calls to fill the opening.

Summit angle:

- Smart Scheduling, waitlist, patient scores, and patient communication can help the team identify who to contact and how to fill the gap.

What to ask:

> What happens today when a high-value appointment cancels?

Scenario 2: A crown claim comes back denied.

What the office feels:

- Billing rework.
- Delayed cash.
- Patient confusion.
- Staff frustration.

Summit angle:

- Claim Review can help flag documentation, narrative, pre-auth, and payer-pattern risks before submission.

What to ask:

> What are the most common claim issues your billing team has to fix after submission?

Scenario 3: The doctor recommends treatment, but the patient does not schedule.

What the office feels:

- Lost production.
- Patient care delayed.
- No consistent follow-up system.

Summit angle:

- Treatment Follow-Up and Payment Plans can help track outreach, patient hesitation, cost concerns, and scheduling status.

What to ask:

> How do you follow up with patients who leave without scheduling recommended treatment?

Scenario 4: The office has a large overdue hygiene list.

What the office feels:

- Empty schedule opportunities.
- Patients drifting away.
- Hygienists underutilized.

Summit angle:

- Recall and Patient Retention can help prioritize outreach instead of treating recall like one giant spreadsheet.

What to ask:

> How do you decide which overdue recall patients to contact first?

Scenario 5: A new front desk hire does not know Open Dental workflows.

What the office feels:

- Manager interruptions.
- Slower check-in and check-out.
- More mistakes.

Summit angle:

- AI Assistant can help with staff workflow questions and Open Dental guidance.

What to ask:

> What questions do new staff ask your manager over and over?

### Pain Ranking Exercise

If a prospect says "everything is a problem," slow them down.

Say:

> I believe you. But for rollout, we still need to rank the pain. If you had to choose the one workflow that would create the fastest win, would it be notes, front desk, billing, recall, treatment follow-up, or reporting?

Then rank:

1. Most painful.
2. Most expensive.
3. Easiest to adopt.
4. Best internal champion.

The best first workflow is usually where pain is high and adoption is realistic.

## 6. Package Playbooks

### Notes Package

#### Plain-English Description

The Notes Package helps providers create cleaner clinical notes faster.

It turns voice or text into a structured draft. The provider reviews, edits, and approves the note.

#### When To Sell It

Sell Notes when the buyer mentions:

- Late charting.
- Documentation backlog.
- Inconsistent notes.
- Provider burnout.
- Need for better SOAP notes.
- Documentation affecting claims.

#### What To Show

Show:

1. AI Notes.
2. A patient note waiting for review.
3. The transcript or input.
4. The SOAP sections.
5. Edit/review/approve workflow.
6. Perio or clinical support only if relevant.

#### Discovery Questions

- "How long do doctors spend finishing notes after the last patient?"
- "Are notes usually completed same day?"
- "Who reviews notes before they are final?"
- "Which procedures create the most documentation?"
- "Do incomplete notes ever delay claims?"

#### Best Talk Track

> Notes are not just paperwork. They affect clinical continuity, claims, risk, and provider time. Summit helps by drafting a structured note that the provider can review and approve.

#### Demo Close

> If notes are the first rollout, success would be simple: fewer unfinished notes, faster provider review, and a cleaner same-day documentation process.

#### Common Objection

"AI notes are risky."

Response:

> They would be risky if AI finalized the chart alone. That is not the workflow. Summit drafts the note. The provider reviews, edits, and approves the final documentation.

## 8. The Sales Call Flow

Use this structure on almost every call.

### Step 1: Set The Frame

Say:

> I will keep this practical. Summit can help with a lot, but I do not want to give you a generic feature tour. I want to understand where your office is feeling the most pressure, then show the workflow that fits.

Why this works:

- It lowers pressure.
- It sounds consultative.
- It gives you permission not to demo everything.

### Step 2: Route The Pain

Ask:

> Where is your team most overloaded right now: notes, front desk work, billing, recall, treatment follow-up, or general practice visibility?

Then listen.

If they say notes, go Notes.

If they say front desk, go Front Desk.

If they say insurance, claims, AR, collections, go Billing.

If they say everything, go Complete but still choose one starting workflow.

### Step 3: Understand Current Process

Ask:

> How are you handling that today?

Then:

- Who owns it?
- How often does it fall behind?
- What happens when it falls behind?
- How do you know it is done?
- What does it cost when it is missed?

### Step 4: Quantify Pain

You do not need perfect numbers. You need rough business impact.

Ask:

- "How many hours a week does that take?"
- "How many people touch that process?"
- "How many patients are affected?"
- "How much money is usually tied up there?"
- "How often does it create a patient complaint?"

### Step 5: Map To Package

Say:

> Based on that, I would not start with every Summit module. I would start with the {{Package}} workflow because {{reason}}.

Example:

> Based on that, I would start with the Billing Package because the biggest issue is not patient communication. It is claim rework, pre-auth tracking, and balances aging past 60 days.

### Step 6: Focused Demo

Show only the relevant path.

Do not say:

> Let me show you everything.

Say:

> I am going to show this through the exact workflow we discussed.

### Step 7: Confirm Fit

Ask:

> Would this workflow actually change how your team handles that problem?

Then:

> What would need to be true for your team to adopt it?

### Step 8: Close For Next Step

Possible next steps:

- Demo with owner.
- Demo with office manager.
- Demo with billing lead.
- Security/integration review.
- Package/pricing discussion.
- Implementation scoping.

Say:

> The next useful step is not another broad overview. It is a focused session with {{stakeholder}} to confirm {{workflow}}, {{integration/security question}}, and the rollout path.

## 11. Objection Handling

### "We already use Open Dental."

Response:

> That makes sense. Summit is not meant to replace Open Dental. Open Dental is the system of record. Summit helps the team act on workflows around that data: notes, huddles, recall, claim review, billing follow-up, treatment follow-up, and reporting.

Follow-up:

> Which Open Dental workflow still takes the most manual effort?

### "We already have reminders."

Response:

> Good. Then reminders may not be the pain. Summit is broader than reminders. It can help with forms, scheduling, recall prioritization, treatment follow-up, balances, claim review, huddles, and staff workflow support.

Follow-up:

> Outside of reminders, where does the front desk still get overloaded?

### "We already have an AI scribe."

Response:

> If notes are fully solved, we should not force the Notes Package. Summit can still help with front desk, billing, treatment follow-up, recall, and practice visibility. If notes are only partly solved, we can compare the workflow.

Follow-up:

> Is documentation the only pain, or are there operational gaps around claims, follow-up, and scheduling too?

### "AI in clinical care sounds risky."

Response:

> I agree that autonomous clinical AI would be risky. Summit should be used as assistive AI. It drafts, flags, organizes, and suggests. The licensed provider makes the final clinical decision.

Follow-up:

> Would you be comfortable evaluating it if provider review and approval are required?

### "Can you guarantee fewer denials?"

Response:

> No. Payers still make coverage decisions, and no responsible vendor should guarantee reimbursement. Summit helps catch common preventable issues before submission, such as missing documentation, frequency issues, payer patterns, and weak narratives.

Follow-up:

> What denial types are costing the team the most time today?

### "How much does it cost?"

Response:

> Pricing depends on the package, provider count, locations, integration scope, and rollout needs. Before we talk numbers, I want to make sure we are matching the package to the workflow that actually matters.

Follow-up:

> Is the primary buying reason notes, front desk capacity, billing, or full practice visibility?

### "We do not have time to implement another system."

Response:

> Then we should not roll out everything at once. The right approach is to pick one painful workflow, assign an owner, define success, and expand only after the team is using it.

Follow-up:

> Which workflow would your team adopt fastest because the pain is already obvious?

### "My staff will resist this."

Response:

> That usually happens when software feels like extra work. Summit should be introduced through a workflow that removes pain quickly, like morning huddle, claim review, or notes awaiting approval.

Follow-up:

> Who on your team would be the most likely champion?

### "Is this HIPAA compliant?"

Response:

> Summit should be evaluated like any healthcare software that may touch PHI. That means BAA, access control, audit logging, security review, consent policies if recording is used, and implementation review. I would not reduce that to a casual yes or no on a sales call.

Follow-up:

> Who handles vendor security and BAA review on your side?

### "Can it replace a front desk person?"

Response:

> I would not position it that way. Summit helps the front desk work from clearer lists and better drafts. It is about capacity and consistency, not removing the human patient relationship.

Follow-up:

> What front desk work is most repetitive today?

### "We are too small."

Response:

> That may be true if there is not enough workflow pain yet. The question is whether you already feel pressure in one area. A small practice with heavy note burden or billing pain can still be a fit. A small practice with no clear bottleneck may not be ready.

Follow-up:

> Which workflow, if any, already feels heavier than it should?

## 13. ROI Made Simple

Do not make fake ROI promises.

Use this formula:

> Value = time saved + revenue recovered + risk reduced + consistency improved.

### Time Saved

Possible time savings:

- Provider charting.
- Huddle prep.
- Front desk list-building.
- Message drafting.
- Claim review.
- Pre-auth tracking.
- Balance follow-up.
- Report gathering.
- Staff questions.

Questions:

- "How many hours per week does this take?"
- "Who does that work?"
- "What is their hourly cost?"
- "What higher-value work could they do instead?"

### Revenue Recovered

Possible revenue recovery:

- Overdue recall scheduled.
- Unaccepted treatment followed up.
- Cancellations filled.
- Claims submitted cleaner.
- Pre-auths followed up.
- Patient balances collected.
- Payment plans offered.
- Fee schedule improved.

- "How many hygiene patients are overdue?"

- Better note review.
- More consistent documentation.

Questions:

- "How do you train new staff?"
- "Where does inconsistency create risk?"

> I would not ask you to trust a generic ROI number. We should pick the workflow first. If it is notes, we estimate provider time saved. If it is recall, we estimate patients reactivated. If it is billing, we estimate rework and AR impact. Then we decide whether the package makes sense.

## 21. Red Flags And Green Flags

### Green Flags

These usually mean the deal is worth real time:

- The prospect can name a specific workflow pain.
- The office manager or owner is involved.
- They already use Open Dental or understand Open Dental workflows.
- They know their recall, AR, claim, or note backlog problem.
- They are willing to pick one starting workflow.
- They care about human review.
- They ask about implementation, security, and training.
- They have an internal champion.

What to do:

> Move toward a focused demo, package recommendation, and rollout discussion.

### Red Flags

These usually mean slow down or disqualify:

- They want AI to make final clinical decisions.
- They want guaranteed claim payment.
- They refuse to discuss HIPAA, BAA, consent, or security.
- They only want the cheapest texting/reminder tool.
- They cannot name any real pain.
- They want every module turned on immediately with no owner.
- They say "our staff will not use anything" and have no plan to change that.
- The only contact is a low-level researcher with no access to decision makers.

What to do:

> Narrow to one workflow. If they still cannot identify pain, nurture instead of pushing.

### The Best Fit Test

Ask:

> If Summit only helped with this one workflow, would it still be worth evaluating?

If yes, keep going.

If no, either the pain is not strong enough or you are selling the wrong workflow.
